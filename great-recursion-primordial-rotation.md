## Abstract

The EFMW Post-156 audit identifies two surviving candidate physical relations while explicitly withholding the status of established laws: a scalar propagation relation and a residual-rotation contribution to a Friedmann-type expansion equation. This paper asks what follows if the latter is not treated merely as a scalar correction but as the norm of a latent oriented rotational degree of freedom. The audited relation

\[
\Delta H^2=\frac{\Omega_U^2}{a^2}
\]

is insensitive to the sign of \(\Omega_U\) and exactly degenerate, within the presently formalized expansion model, with a curvature shift. We reconstruct the scalar \(\Omega_U^2\) as the contraction \(\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\boldsymbol\Omega\) of a candidate rotation vector and derive longitudinal and transverse observables that retain orientation. This yields a concrete route for breaking both sign and curvature degeneracies without claiming that primordial rotation has been observed. The resulting hypothesis is that the earliest physically meaningful state may satisfy \(\boldsymbol\Omega\neq0\), so that cosmic evolution begins not from an orientation-free scalar state but from an already dynamical, oriented field state. We call this conceptual alternative the **Great Recursion**. It is presented here as a falsifiable research program, not as an established replacement for Big-Bang cosmology.

## 1. Motivation

The Post-156 EFMW theorem and Zoo audit sharply limits the physical claims that can presently be defended. Its strongest surviving candidate relations are

\[
(1-\alpha^2)\omega^2=c^2k^2,
\qquad
v_\phi=\frac{c}{\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}},
\]

and

\[
\Delta H^2=\frac{\Omega_U^2}{a^2}.
\]

The first describes a candidate scalar propagation sector. The second describes a candidate residual-rotation contribution to cosmological expansion. Formal verification establishes consequences of the stipulated mathematical model; it does not establish that either relation is a law of nature.

The second relation nevertheless poses a structural question. Why does rotation appear only through its square? A squared rotational quantity retains magnitude while erasing orientation. If the scalar term is the contraction of a more primitive oriented object, the current cosmological equation may be observing only part of the underlying structure.

This paper develops that possibility.

## 2. What the audited relation actually says

Start with

\[
\Delta H^2=\frac{\Omega_U^2}{a^2}.
\]

Multiplication by \(a^2\) gives

\[
a^2\Delta H^2=\Omega_U^2.
\]

The immediate prediction is non-negativity:

\[
\Delta H^2\ge0.
\]

But

\[
(+\Omega_U)^2=(-\Omega_U)^2,
\]

so expansion history cannot recover rotational orientation. This is the sign degeneracy identified in the Post-156 audit.

The audit also establishes a stronger identifiability problem. With the curvature contribution written as \(-k/a^2\),

\[
-\frac{k}{a^2}+\frac{\Omega_U^2}{a^2}
=-\frac{k-\Omega_U^2}{a^2}.
\]

Defining

\[
k_{\mathrm{eff}}=k-\Omega_U^2
\]

gives

\[
H_{\mathrm{EFMW}}^2(k,\Omega_U)
=H_{\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}}^2(k_{\mathrm{eff}}).
\]

Within this model, expansion history alone therefore cannot distinguish residual rotation from curvature. This is a genuine falsification constraint: an independent observable is required.

## 3. Reconstructing an oriented rotational degree of freedom

We now introduce an explicit hypothesis, not contained in the audited scalar equation: suppose \(\Omega_U^2\) is the scalar norm of an oriented quantity

\[
\boldsymbol\Omega.
\]

Then

\[
\Omega_U^2
\longrightarrow
\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\boldsymbol\Omega
=\|\boldsymbol\Omega\|^2,
\]

and the original relation is recovered as

\[
\boxed{
\Delta H^2=
\frac{\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\boldsymbol\Omega}{a^2}
}.
\]

This reconstruction changes no scalar prediction. Its purpose is to preserve information discarded by the contraction.

Under orientation reversal,

\[
\boldsymbol\Omega\rightarrow-\boldsymbol\Omega,
\]

while

\[
\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\boldsymbol\Omega
\rightarrow
\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\boldsymbol\Omega.
\]

The scalar cosmological term is therefore necessarily blind to handedness.

## 4. Longitudinal and transverse observables

Let \(\mathbf n\) be a unit direction associated with an independently measurable physical observable. Define the signed longitudinal projection

\[
L_{\mathbf n}
=\frac{\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\mathbf n}{a}.
\]

Under reversal of the proposed cosmic rotation,

\[
L_{\mathbf n}\rightarrow-L_{\mathbf n}.
\]

Unlike the Friedmann contribution, this quantity retains orientation.

A transverse response can likewise be defined by

\[
\mathbf T_{\mathbf n}
=\frac{\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf n}{a}.
\]

It also reverses sign when \(\boldsymbol\Omega\) reverses. Its magnitude is

\[
T_{\mathbf n}
=\frac{\|\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf n\|}{a}.
\]

For a general physical vector \(\mathbf v\), the corresponding rotational action is

\[
\mathbf A_{\rm rot}
=\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf v,
\]

with

\[
\|\mathbf A_{\rm rot}\|
=\Omega v|\sin\theta|.
\]

A natural nondegeneracy condition at an initial reference surface is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|\boldsymbol\Omega_0\times\mathbf v_0\|>0
}.
\]

This states that the proposed rotational degree of freedom acts nontrivially on at least one physical direction.

## 5. Reassembling the scalar relation

For unit \(\mathbf n\), ordinary vector decomposition gives

\[
\|\boldsymbol\Omega\|^2
=(\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\mathbf n)^2
+\|\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf n\|^2.
\]

Dividing by \(a^2\),

\[
\boxed{
\Delta H^2=L_{\mathbf n}^2+T_{\mathbf n}^2
}.
\]

Thus the original scalar residual-rotation term can be interpreted, under the vector hypothesis, as an orientation-blind contraction of longitudinal and transverse rotational components.

This identity is mathematical rather than empirical. Its significance is methodological: it tells us what additional measurements would be required to reconstruct the hypothesized oriented quantity.

## 6. Breaking the curvature-rotation degeneracy

Expansion measurements constrain

\[
k_{\rm eff}=k-\|\boldsymbol\Omega\|^2.
\]

They cannot determine the two terms separately.

Suppose, however, that independent directional measurements determine the projections of \(\boldsymbol\Omega\) along sufficiently many independent axes. Then \(\boldsymbol\Omega\) can in principle be reconstructed, yielding

\[
\|\boldsymbol\Omega\|^2.
\]

The curvature parameter would consequently be recovered from

\[
\boxed{
k=k_{\rm eff}+\|\boldsymbol\Omega\|^2}.
\]

This supplies a direct experimental strategy. The crucial observation would not merely be a nonzero scalar excess in \(H^2\). It would be a consistent **signed orientation** independently recovered from multiple observables.

Curvature can imitate the scalar \(\Omega^2/a^2\) contribution in the audited equation. Scalar curvature alone cannot provide a common signed rotation vector across independent directional measurements.

## 7. The initial-state hypothesis

The strongest form of the proposal is not that the present formalism proves rotation at mathematical \(t=0\). It does not.

Instead define the primordial-rotation hypothesis as

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{t\rightarrow0^+}\boldsymbol\Omega(t)\neq0
}.
\]

This formulation avoids pretending that the presently effective Friedmann equation remains valid at the classical singular boundary.

Indeed, if \(\Omega_U\) is constant while \(a\rightarrow0\), then

\[
\frac{\Omega_U^2}{a^2}\rightarrow\infty.
\]

That behavior forces a choice among physically distinct possibilities: the effective equation may fail before the boundary; \(\Omega(t)\) may evolve nontrivially; the initial state may require a nonsingular completion; or the proposed residual rotation may simply be absent. These alternatives are part of the hypothesis's falsifiable content rather than details to be hidden.

## 8. From Big Bang to Great Recursion

The conventional shorthand picture is

\[
\text{hot early state}\rightarrow\text{expansion}\rightarrow\text{structure}.
\]

The hypothesis developed here instead permits an initial state containing an oriented dynamical degree of freedom:

\[
\text{oriented initial field state}
\rightarrow
\{\text{expansion, propagation, rotation}\}
\rightarrow
\text{evolving fields}
\rightarrow
\text{structure}.
\]

We call this conceptual model the **Great Recursion**.

The term does not mean that the universe has been shown to be cyclic, nor does it by itself imply a universe before the observable universe. Rather, it denotes a change in emphasis: the beginning is treated not as an orientation-free instantaneous event but as a dynamical boundary condition whose propagation and rotational degrees of freedom participate recursively in subsequent evolution.

In this interpretation, the candidate scalar propagation relation and the reconstructed rotational sector suggest a possible pairing:

\[
\boxed{
\text{propagation} + \text{oriented rotation}
}
\]

rather than a proliferation of independent fundamental laws.

Whether that pairing reflects nature is an experimental question.

## 9. Falsifiable consequences

The Great Recursion hypothesis gains scientific content only through observations capable of rejecting it. The most important targets are:

1. **Directional null test.** If increasingly precise rotation-sensitive observations find no common orientation, bounds on \(\|\boldsymbol\Omega\|\) tighten and the nonzero primordial-vector hypothesis is constrained.

2. **Sign consistency.** Independent observables sensitive linearly to \(\boldsymbol\Omega\) should recover mutually compatible handedness and direction after coordinate conventions and systematics are controlled.

3. **Scalar-vector consistency.** A reconstructed vector must satisfy

   \[
   \Delta H^2=\frac{\|\boldsymbol\Omega\|^2}{a^2}
   \]

   wherever the effective cosmological relation is asserted to apply.

4. **Curvature separation.** Independent curvature measurements combined with directional rotation measurements must reproduce the expansion-inferred

   \[
   k_{\rm eff}=k-\|\boldsymbol\Omega\|^2.
   \]

5. **Redshift/evolution law.** A complete theory must specify \(\boldsymbol\Omega(a)\), rather than simply assume constant rotation. Different evolution laws produce distinguishable histories.

6. **Primordial signatures.** If the vector survives from an early epoch, a viable model should identify specific parity-, handedness-, or preferred-direction observables and quantitatively predict their amplitudes.

Failure of these consistency conditions would reject or sharply restrict the proposed reconstruction.

## 10. Relationship to the scalar propagation candidate

The other surviving Post-156 candidate relation is

\[
(1-\alpha^2)\omega^2=c^2k^2,
\]

or

\[
v_\phi=\frac{c}{\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}}.
\]

The audit establishes the real-wave domain

\[
\alpha^2<1
\]

for nontrivial real propagation and gives the inverse mapping

\[
\alpha^2=1-\left(\frac{c}{v_\phi}\right)^2.
\]

It does not select a unique nonzero \(\alpha\).

Likewise, the residual-rotation relation does not select a unique nonzero \(\Omega_U\). The present framework therefore supplies **functional predictions and identifiability structure**, not numerical values for new universal constants.

That limitation is scientifically useful. It tells us precisely what additional theoretical constraint or empirical measurement is needed.

## 11. Status of the proposal

Three levels must remain separate.

**Kernel-checked/model-level results:** the Post-156 audit formally checks the specified mathematical consequences, including sign and curvature-rotation degeneracies.

**Reconstruction proposed here:** replacing the scalar \(\Omega_U^2\) by \(\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\boldsymbol\Omega\) and introducing signed longitudinal/transverse observables is a hypothesis designed to expose otherwise discarded orientation information.

**Empirical claim:** no result presented here establishes that the universe possesses a primordial rotation vector, that \(\boldsymbol\Omega(0)\neq0\), or that the Great Recursion describes physical reality.

Those propositions require independent observational evidence.

## 12. Conclusion

The EFMW Post-156 residual-rotation relation contains less information than an oriented rotational state would contain. Its dependence on \(\Omega_U^2\) necessarily erases handedness and, within the currently formalized Friedmann expression, produces an exact degeneracy with curvature.

Breaking the scalar quantity into an underlying vector norm suggests a simple experimental program: search for observables linear in an oriented \(\boldsymbol\Omega\), reconstruct the vector independently of expansion history, and test whether its squared norm accounts for the scalar cosmological contribution while independently measured curvature satisfies the required consistency relation.

If those tests fail, the Great Recursion hypothesis is constrained or rejected. If multiple independent observables instead reconstruct the same nonzero primordial orientation and satisfy the scalar consistency equations, the result would demand a substantially different account of cosmic initial conditions.

The proposed paradigm change is therefore not the statement that the Big Bang has already been replaced. It is a testable question:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Was the earliest physical state already moving, oriented, and rotating?}
}
\]

If the answer is experimentally yes, cosmic origin is better described not merely as an expansion from an initial state, but as the evolution of an already dynamical one—the **Great Recursion**.
