# EFMW Quantum Gravity: From Quantum Connectivity to a Microscopic Constraint Obstruction

## A bounded research synthesis of QST-001 through QG-030

## Abstract

This work investigates whether microscopic quantum connectivity can produce effective gravitational geometry in the EFMW research program. A sequence of finite quantum graph, canonical tensor, classical Einstein–matter, and perturbative quantum-gravity laboratories separates properties that can be constructed from those that must emerge. The early experiments reveal failures of ordinary elastic and compatible-link models to produce an exclusive massless spin-2 sector or preserve gravitational constraints. Later laboratories reconstruct two physical tensor polarizations, linearized gauge constraints, reciprocal classical Einstein–scalar dynamics, quantum curvature microcausality, tree-level gravitational Compton scattering and scalar–scalar graviton emission. Numerical radiation amplitudes approach the first three orders of the standard soft-graviton expansion. A leading-soft coherent model exhibits infrared-inclusive normalization, and an independently formulated angular/partial-wave comparison verifies a regulated two-mass elastic unitarity cut. These are deliberately conventional reference models, not deductions from EFMW.

The central mathematical result is an exact obstruction: a strictly positive-definite quadratic canonical Hamiltonian cannot preserve a nontrivial linear first-class constraint surface. Finite quadratic constraint penalties do not change that closure defect. The result is established algebraically and illustrated by 512 positive-definite examples and 64 semidefinite transverse-traceless controls. It identifies why the tested ordinary oscillator/link architecture cannot by itself supply exact gravitational gauge symmetry. It does not rule out constrained, singular, nonlinear, noncanonical, or controlled low-energy emergence mechanisms. The next decisive objective is a microscopic derivation of the effective gravitational constraint algebra and matter coupling, with a prediction distinguishable from conventional gravity.

**Keywords:** emergent gravity; EFMW; first-class constraints; quadratic Hamiltonians; spin-2; gravitational radiation; soft gravitons; infrared consistency; computational verification.

## 1. Research question and method

The central question is not whether familiar gravitational equations can be evaluated on a computer. It is whether a specified microscopic EFMW dynamics can produce the physical and gauge structure that makes those equations appropriate in the first place.

The research therefore separates three levels:

1. **Microscopic candidates:** finite quantum connectivity, reciprocal matter–geometry models, graph transitions and canonical link variables.
2. **Reference constructions:** prescribed classical or quantum gravitational dynamics used to establish target identities, physical degrees of freedom, correspondence and failure controls.
3. **Emergence:** a derived connection between levels 1 and 2, including the effective constraint algebra, relativistic propagation, universal matter coupling and a discriminating physical prediction.

Passing a reference benchmark does not establish level 3. The experiments are a chain of related but distinct mathematical models, not a single continuously evolved Hamiltonian. Different reductions, regulators, normalizations and sources occur along the chain. Numerical tolerances are model-specific. Negative controls deliberately remove necessary terms or change assumptions; they test a specified calculation rather than every alternative theory.

The early QG-003–013 experiments are summarized from the [interim research report][interim]. Their original complete numerical archives were not publicly deposited with that paper and are not independently replayed in this synthesis. QG-014–025 have published reference records in the [Quantum-Gravity repository][repo]. QG-026–030 have local reproducibility packages identified in Section 10; their new source and raw evidence are not uploaded by this main-paper publication. All results below are conditional on the stated models.

## 2. From quantum connectivity to constrained geometry

The original QST-001 laboratory juxtaposes a conventional Einstein–scalar/FLRW model with a separate finite 888-state unitary ring. The gravitational action is supplied, not derived from the finite system. A prescribed ring has effectively one-dimensional propagation in its appropriate scaling window; higher-dimensional product geometry requires additional structure. Assigning distance from mutual information or allowing graph coherence does not automatically produce a Lorentzian spacetime.

The early investigations successively test quantum matter–mode backreaction, propagation on prescribed networks, vacuum-energy-informed graph rewiring, superposed graph connectivity and graph-basis decoherence. In the reported finite experiments, quantum transitions enable reciprocal effects and interference. However, no stable spontaneously generated three-dimensional continuum phase was established, and suppression of graph interference did not imply selection of a unique classical geometry.

The tensor investigations sharpen the problem. Scalar propagation can become approximately relativistic without acquiring spin two. Ordinary displacement strain is longitudinal in the continuum and has vanishing transverse-traceless (TT) projection. Independent axial and diagonal link variables can provide sufficient components to represent a symmetric tensor, but representation alone does not remove extra physical modes. The tested compatible-link quadratic model retains six gapless tensor-coordinate modes rather than only two physical graviton modes. Finite penalties and a compatibility map do not supply exact gravitational first-class constraints.

For a nonzero spatial momentum $q$, the linearized ADM reference uses six independent symmetric metric components $h_{ij}$ and six conjugate components $\pi_{ij}$, with four independent linear first-class constraints proportional to

$$
C_0(q)=q_iq_jh_{ij}-|q|^2\operatorname{tr}h,
\qquad C_i(q)=q_j\pi_{ij}.
$$

The four constraints and four corresponding gauge directions reduce the twelve-dimensional real canonical phase space of the mode to four physical dimensions, or two canonical pairs. At nonzero momentum these pairs can be represented by the TT polarizations. This count assumes the usual linearized reference constraints and excludes the homogeneous mode. It is not a derivation of the constraints from a quantum graph.

QG-014 found local rank-two gauge constructions with two physical modes but nonrelativistic quadratic or cubic dispersion. QG-015 instead discretized linearized ADM using staggered local operators, producing two physical polarizations and infrared relativistic dispersion. Its constraint structure is imposed through the gravitational reference. That distinction motivates the obstruction theorem in Section 7.

It is not a generic three-dimensional numerical-relativity solver or a black-hole formation calculation.

QG-019 tests quantum-mediator and expectation-value diagnostics using an oscillator and a separate static Poisson model. QG-020 quantizes the two physical modes of the staggered linearized tensor field. QG-021 studies mobile quantum particles interacting through an instantaneous reduced Newtonian potential, and QG-022 adds two retained TT modes to a finite self-adjoint Hamiltonian. These different models are not an operator-valued solution of the full nonlinear Einstein constraints.

QG-023 reconstructs covariant weak-field scalar exchange from the Einstein–scalar action. The on-shell scalar stress vertex satisfies the leading Ward identity, the tree numerator is Lorentz invariant, and the static Born limit is the conventional Newtonian potential. A separate conserved classical quadrupole source supplies a retarded-response test; it is not the same quantum scattering configuration. The study detects trace omissions, double-counted Newtonian attraction and nonretarded controls.

### Quantum gravitational causality (QG-024)

For free linearized gravity the local electric curvature component is $E_{ij}=R^{(1)}_{0i0j}$. Its $xx$ commutator is proportional to $(\partial_y^2+\partial_z^2)^2$ acting on the massless scalar Pauli–Jordan commutator. Local differentiation does not enlarge causal support. Smearing two such curvature observables over compact disjoint regions permits a direct sender/readout no-signaling test at spacelike separation.

| Momentum cutoff | Maximum normalized spacelike curvature commutator |
|---:|---:|
| 16 | 5.34947e-2 |
| 32 | 1.34083e-4 |
| 48 | 2.10621e-9 |
| 64 | 4.20101e-8 |
| 96 | 3.59404e-10 |

The finest box sum used 3,705,092 nonzero wavevectors. The maximum spacelike qubit trace distance was $7.92933\times10^{-11}$; an independently formulated continuum radial integral gave a spacelike commutator residual of $4.83758\times10^{-17}$. Refinement is not monotonic because of the smearing Fourier transform. Nonzero spacelike vacuum covariance is compatible with vanishing signaling. A spatially nonlocal bare-TT field serves as a comparator, not a local physical signal. All twelve programmed diagnostics passed in the reference report.

The result establishes the tested component of free curvature microcausality, not nonlinear gauge-dressed matter causality or entanglement harvesting. See [QG-024][qg024] for smearing, normalization, regulators and operational assumptions.

## 4. Nonlinear tree-level quantum interactions

### 4.1 Gravitational Compton scattering (QG-025)

For a graviton scattering from minimally coupled massive scalar matter, the four tree contributions are the s-channel and u-channel scalar exchanges, the two-graviton scalar contact term, and the graviton exchange with the cubic Einstein vertex. The cubic vertex is essential to the complete gauge-consistent amplitude in the chosen representation.

The explicit four-diagram construction from the conventional action was compared against the known factorized Compton expression over 128 randomized kinematic events and 512 helicity cases.

| Diagnostic | Maximum normalized residual |
|---|---:|
| Four-diagram versus factorized amplitude | 2.42111e-13 |
| Field-strength formulation | 1.31381e-14 |
| Lorentz invariance | 2.43887e-13 |
| Gauge-shift invariance | 7.59494e-13 |
| Incoming Ward identity | 1.05281e-12 |
| Outgoing Ward identity | 1.47320e-12 |
| Differential cross-section agreement | 7.09433e-14 |

Removing an individual contribution produced a detectable gauge defect in 500/512 s-channel, 492/512 u-channel, 511/512 contact, and 511/512 graviton-exchange cases at the reported threshold. Individual diagrams are gauge-dependent; these are mechanism-removal diagnostics, not independently measurable contributions.

The tested unpolarized differential cross section in the scalar rest frame agrees with

$$
\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}
=G^2m^2\left(\frac{\omega'}{\omega}\right)^2
\frac{\cos^8(\theta/2)+\sin^8(\theta/2)}{\sin^4(\theta/2)}.
$$

All seventeen programmed checks passed. The local run reports byte-identical numerical replay after a serialization-only repair. The published [QG-025 benchmark][qg025] contains the reference code and report; its raw JSON outputs remain in the local archive. No nonminimal coupling, loop identity or EFMW microscopic mechanism is established.

### 4.2 Quantum gravitational radiation (QG-026)

The next reference evaluates emission from two distinguishable massive scalars,

$$
\phi_A+\phi_B\longrightarrow\phi_A+\phi_B+g.
$$

The full tree calculation contains two external-line emission contributions and one scalar contact contribution for each species, together with the cubic graviton contribution. It uses recoiling final-state momenta. No soft-factorized answer is substituted into the full seven-diagram amplitude.

Across 96 hard geometries, seven emitted-energy fractions and two conjugate helicities (1,344 helicity cases), all twenty-one programmed checks passed. The maximum normalized Ward, gauge-shift and Lorentz-boost residuals were respectively $8.04068\times10^{-16}$, $8.12925\times10^{-16}$ and $2.99633\times10^{-16}$. The absolute Ward residual reaches $1.02066\times10^{-8}$ at the smallest frequency, reflecting cancellation of individually large terms. The normalization is a cancellation-scale diagnostic.

For conserved hard momenta $p_i$, incoming/outgoing signs $\eta_i$, and soft null momentum $k$, the amplitude approaches Weinberg's leading factor [1]:

$$
\mathcal M_5\sim\frac{\kappa}{2}
\sum_i\eta_i\frac{p_i^\mu p_i^\nu\epsilon_{\mu\nu}}{p_i\cdot k}
\mathcal M_4,\qquad\kappa^2=32\pi G .
$$

The relative ensemble error in the frequency-scaled amplitude falls from .2692603 at emission fraction .25 to .000105628 at .0001, with fitted exponent 1.000209. The conditional emitted-energy spectrum approaches the zero-frequency asymptotic-stress prediction with relative ensemble error .000154981. An angular integral evaluated both by TT quadrature and by a pairwise rapidity expression agrees to $2.46648\times10^{-13}$.

The leading soft gauge identity reduces to conservation of hard momentum for universal coupling. Changing one species' emission coefficient by 25% produces a maximum gauge residual .879948 instead of $3.55271\times10^{-15}$ for common coupling, in the stated dimensionless normalization. This illustrates the conventional universality argument; universal coupling is already assumed in the starting action. It is not a new equivalence-principle prediction.

The radiation comparison is a zero-frequency correspondence for a fixed hard angular bin, not an absolute calibrated energy per collision, finite-time gravitational waveform or a new material/gravitational mechanism. The full report and source are in the QG-026 local evidence package.

## 5. Subleading radiation and infrared consistency

### 5.1 Soft expansion through sub-subleading order (QG-027)

The first three orders of the minimally coupled scalar tree soft expansion are tested against the QG-026 seven-diagram amplitude, following the standard orbital soft operators [1–3]. Let $x_i$ be the four all-outgoing hard momenta at the radiative configuration, $k=\omega N$, and $e$ a null transverse complex helicity vector, with the graviton tensor $\epsilon=e\otimes e$. Define

$$
a_i=x_i\cdot e,\quad b_i=x_i\cdot N,\quad
d_i=a_iN-b_ie,\quad D_i=d_i\cdot\partial_{x_i}.
$$

For an extended stripped hard amplitude $M$, with the coupling set to one, the prediction for $\omega\mathcal M_5$ is

$$
\sum_i\left[
\frac{a_i^2}{2b_i}M+
\omega\frac{a_i}{2b_i}D_iM+
\omega^2\frac{D_i^2M}{4b_i}
\right].
$$

At finite recoil the four hard momenta do not sum to zero. Two explicit smooth continuations of the Born amplitude away from its momentum-conserving locus are compared, with exchange denominators defined from the two sides. They agree on the conserving locus but need not agree term by term away from it. Derivatives are evaluated algebraically from the rational denominator and affine numerator, not fitted to the full radiative amplitude.

The study uses 96 fresh geometries, two recoil paths, five soft fractions, and two conjugate helicities: 1,920 amplitude cases. Helicity conjugates are not independent random geometries. The table reports aggregate error exponents for the scaled amplitude, fitted over the four smallest fractions.

| Recoil / hard continuation | Leading | Through subleading | Through sub-subleading |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Fixed / q | 1.000868 | 2.002063 | 3.001077 |
| Fixed / r | 1.001275 | 2.002647 | 3.008767 |
| Rotating / q | 1.001676 | 2.002522 | 3.004722 |
| Rotating / r | 1.002107 | 2.003269 | 3.011468 |

For the rotating q case at fraction .0003, the aggregate errors are $2.50570\times10^{-4}$, $5.22810\times10^{-8}$ and $1.00209\times10^{-11}$ for the successive truncations. All four recoil/continuation gates pass. Omitting a higher-order term leaves the lower-order scaling. This tests known tree-level identities for minimal scalar gravity; it does not extend the theorem to arbitrary hard spin, nonminimal interactions or loop corrections.

### 5.2 Leading-soft inclusive normalization (QG-028)

The angular-integrated soft coefficient $B$ from QG-026 supplies the idealized leading-mode occupation density $dN=B\,d\omega/\omega$. With lower cutoff $\lambda$, upper soft bound $\Lambda$, and detector threshold $\Delta$, the coherent model assigns unresolved and resolved mean occupations

$$
N_u=B\log(\Delta/\lambda),\qquad N_r=B\log(\Lambda/\Delta).
$$

The zero-emission probability is $\exp[-(N_u+N_r)]$. Summing over all unresolved counts gives the probability of no resolved emissions,

$$
P(n_r=0)=e^{-(N_u+N_r)}
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{N_u^n}{n!}=e^{-N_r}.
$$

Sixteen frozen hard configurations, two coefficient scales, and four infrared cutoffs produce 128 configurations. Explicit binned count convolution and Poisson thinning agree to total variation below $1.9\times10^{-15}$. The maximum variation of $P(n_r=0)$ under cutoff changes is $1.887\times10^{-15}$. A compound-Poisson energy characteristic-function check improves from .0003459752 to .0000215288 with bin refinement. Removing unresolved vacuum compensation causes divergence exceeding $1.08\times10^8$ in the strongest stress case.

The factor-of-50 coefficient scale is a numerical stress test, not a physical perturbative regime. The cancellation is built into the coherent displacement normalization, and the hard process is held fixed without exact many-emission recoil or a total-energy bound. This is a leading-soft real–virtual structural model [1], not a direct computation of virtual gravitational Feynman diagrams, Coulomb phases, or an all-orders inclusive cross section.

## 6. Regulated elastic unitarity cut (QG-029)

For distinguishable scalar masses in the center-of-momentum frame, use the real Born gravitational exchange amplitude $A(c)$ as a function of scattering-angle cosine $c$. To obtain finite angular integrals, replace its exchange denominator by $2p^2(1-c+\delta)$ with positive $\delta$. This ad hoc angular regulator is not asserted to preserve the full covariant gravitational gauge theory.

The two-mass elastic intermediate-state cut is

$$
\operatorname{Im} M_{\mathrm{cut}}(c)=
\frac{p}{32\pi^2W}\int d\Omega_u\,
A(n_i\cdot u)A(u\cdot n_f),
$$

where $W$ is the total center-of-momentum energy. If

$$
a_\ell=\frac12\int_{-1}^{1}A(x)P_\ell(x)\,dx,
$$

the angular convolution equals

$$
4\pi\sum_\ell(2\ell+1)a_\ell^2P_\ell(c).
$$

Direct angular quadrature and Legendre decomposition were compared for three kinematic configurations, three finite regulators, and three scattering angles (27 cases).

| Diagnostic | Maximum relative discrepancy |
|---|---:|
| Born numerator reconstruction | 1.5614e-15 |
| Direct / partial-wave cut | 4.8755e-13 |
| Forward optical-theorem normalization | 2.6356e-13 |

The forward identity is $\operatorname{Im}M_{\mathrm{cut}}(1)=2pW\sigma_{\mathrm{el}}$ in the chosen normalization. The six programmed checks passed after a JSON boolean conversion repair, with the original failed attempt retained.

This benchmark evaluates one regulated absorptive contribution from the on-shell cut. The optical-theorem normalization and positivity checks are properties of that prescribed construction. No independently computed complete one-loop amplitude is compared; no real part, covariant regulator removal, crossed or massless cuts, renormalization or all-orders unitarity is established. A complete quantum-gravity loop calculation remains open in this research sequence.

## 7. Principal mathematical result: the quadratic constraint obstruction (QG-030)

**Theorem (scoped no-go for exact linear first-class constraint preservation).** Let $z\in\mathbb R^{2n}$ be real canonical phase-space coordinates with invertible standard symplectic matrix $J$, $J^T=-J$. Let $C$ be a real $m\times 2n$ full-row-rank matrix with $m>0$, defining linear constraints $Cz=0$ that satisfy $CJC^T=0$. Let

$$
H(z)=\tfrac12 z^TKz,\qquad K=K^T\succ0.
$$

Then the Hamiltonian flow cannot leave the entire constraint surface $\ker C$ invariant.

**Proof.** Hamilton's equations give $\dot z=JKz$. Invariance of the linear constraint surface would require $CJKv=0$ for every $v\in\ker C$. Any linear map that vanishes on $\ker C$ factors through $C$, so there is an $m\times m$ matrix $A$ such that

$$
CJK=AC .
$$

Right multiplication by $JC^T$, using the first-class condition, gives

$$
CJKJC^T=ACJC^T=0.
$$

Define $L=JC^T$. Because $J$ is invertible and $C$ has full row rank, $L$ has full column rank. Antisymmetry of $J$ implies

$$
L^TKL=-CJKJC^T=0.
$$

But strict positivity of $K$ implies $u^TL^TKLu=(Lu)^TK(Lu)>0$ for every nonzero $u\in\mathbb R^m$. This contradicts $L^TKL=0$. Hence $\ker C$ cannot be invariant. $\square$

**Corollary 1 (semidefinite case).** If $K\succeq0$ rather than $K\succ0$, invariance requires $KJC^T=0$: write $L^TKL=(K^{1/2}L)^T(K^{1/2}L)$ and use the vanishing Gram matrix. Conversely, $KJC^T=0$ implies $CJK=0$ by transposition. In this positive-semidefinite quadratic class, exact preservation is therefore equivalent to the gauge directions lying in the nullspace of the quadratic form. Positive energy on the reduced physical sector remains possible.

**Corollary 2 (finite penalty obstruction).** Adding a quadratic constraint penalty changes $K$ to $K_\lambda=K+\lambda C^TC$, but

$$
CJK_\lambda=CJK+\lambda CJC^TC=CJK.
$$

Consequently, no finite penalty of this form repairs a pre-existing exact linear first-class closure defect, although approximate low-energy behavior may improve under additional assumptions.

These statements are direct symplectic-algebra deductions under explicit hypotheses; no priority or novelty claim about the underlying general mathematical observation is made. The contribution here is its use to diagnose the specific EFMW oscillator/link emergence route.

### 7.1 Numerical challenge and positive controls

The QG-030 test constructs the four normalized linear ADM constraints for each of 64 randomly oriented nonzero wavevectors in the twelve-dimensional canonical metric/momentum phase space. Two positive quadratic families are tested: compatible axial/diagonal link oscillators with positive link weights, and general positive-definite random quadratic forms. Each is challenged at penalty strengths 0, 1, 100 and 10,000, giving 512 cases. A separate 64-case semidefinite TT-only control imposes the physical tensor projector explicitly.

For orthonormal constraint rows, $P=I-C^TC$ projects onto $\ker C$. The numerical closure defect is the spectral norm $\|CJKP\|_2$.

### 7.2 What the obstruction does not establish

The result does not forbid positive energy on a physical quotient, quantization after constraint reduction, constrained or singular Lagrangians, indefinite quadratic forms on an auxiliary space, nonlinear constraint surfaces, noncanonical graph dynamics, or gauge symmetry that arises only in a controlled continuum/low-energy limit. It does not show that gravity cannot emerge from quantum information, and it is not a no-go theorem for EFMW as a whole.

Exact microscopic constraint preservation and approximate dynamical protection are different obligations. Earlier QG-013 results illustrate why a finite energy gap can suppress static leakage without preventing coherent phase errors or resonant transitions. A model that claims emergent gauge symmetry must specify its approximation, scaling limit and error bounds, rather than infer exact gauge symmetry from a finite penalty or good short-time fidelity.

## 9. Successor research program: the emergence gate

The closing experiment is a stopping criterion for the tested positive-quadratic route, not a claim to have exhausted quantum gravity.

The next research decision must specify a different microscopic mechanism. A successor model should define its microscopic configuration space, Hilbert space or canonical variables, physical observables, Hamiltonian and scaling limit without presupposing the sought continuum equations. If a physical constraint sector is imposed, that assumption must be identified; if it is claimed to emerge, the map and error control must be derived.

A meaningful first gate is the derivation of effective constraints with the correct two-polarization relativistic infrared sector, including their closure under the microscopic dynamics. A subsequent gate is the nonlinear algebra: schematically the classical hypersurface-deformation relation includes a metric-dependent structure function,

$$
\{H[N],H[M]\}
=D[h^{ij}(N\partial_j M-M\partial_jN)].
$$

A quantum successor must define the corresponding operators, domains, ordering and regulator, and test closure with anomaly and mechanism-removal controls. Matching the linearized Abelian algebra is not enough to establish this nonlinear relation. Matter must couple through a derived conserved stress-energy structure with a consistent low-energy equivalence principle, not through independently fitted forces. The correspondence program must retain valid causal observables, tree amplitudes and radiation limits, and address complete loop consistency using a suitable effective-field-theory treatment [4].

Finally, a claim of physical novelty requires an independently calculable difference from the conventional reference: a defined observable, domain of validity, parameter calibration, null model, and prospective falsification criterion. External reproduction and empirical comparison are separate milestones.

The scientific endpoint of the present investigation is therefore not a completed theory. It is a restriction on the class of mechanisms that can plausibly supply one.

## 11. Conclusion

The EFMW quantum-gravity investigation has progressed from finite graph and oscillator models to detailed conventional gravitational benchmarks, including classical constrained dynamics, quantized free tensor modes, tree-level gravitational self-interactions and radiation, subleading soft behavior, leading-soft infrared normalization and a regulated elastic unitarity cut.

These tests establish targets, not microscopic emergence. The most consequential result is the mathematical obstruction to exact linear first-class constraint preservation by strictly positive-definite quadratic canonical Hamiltonians. It explains the failure of the specified ordinary oscillator architecture and proves why finite quadratic penalties alone cannot repair that exact closure defect.

The next advance must establish a microscopic mechanism with an emergent gravitational physical sector and effective constraint algebra, then test its consequences against established gravity and independent evidence. The work is a bounded research program with a precise unresolved theoretical frontier—not a completed quantum theory of gravity.
